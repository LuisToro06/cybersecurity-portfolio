# Initial Security Awareness Risk Assessment

## 1. Objective

Identify common human-related cybersecurity risk areas that can be addressed through security awareness and education activities.

## 2. Scope

This assessment represents a controlled educational assessment of common cybersecurity awareness risk areas.

It does not represent an assessment of a real organization's users, systems, or security posture.

## 3. Risk Assessment Matrix

| Risk Area | Threat | Potential Human Behavior | Potential Impact | Likelihood | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phishing | Credential theft | Clicking suspicious links or providing information | High | High | High |
| Password Reuse | Account compromise | Reusing passwords across services | High | High | High |
| Social Engineering | Unauthorized access | Trusting impersonation or manipulation | High | Medium | High |
| Information Exposure | Data disclosure | Sharing sensitive information improperly | High | Medium | High |
| Unsafe Browsing | Malicious content or fraud | Visiting suspicious websites or downloading unsafe content | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| Incident Reporting | Delayed response | Failing to report suspicious activity | Medium | Medium | Medium |

## 4. Risk Interpretation

The matrix identifies phishing, password security, social engineering, information protection, safe browsing, and incident reporting as relevant awareness topics for the educational program.

The prioritization is used to organize the laboratory activities and does not represent measured risk in a real organization.

## 5. Awareness Response

The identified risk areas will be addressed through:

- Security awareness materials.
- Scenario-based exercises.
- Knowledge assessments.
- Secure behavior recommendations.
- Incident reporting guidance.
- Continuous improvement activities.
